# Hangxiety: Why You Feel Anxious the Day After Drinking

If you feel keyed up, guilty, shaky, or unusually worried after drinking, start here. You do not need to solve every thought this morning. Your body may be under extra strain, and a small physical reset can give your nervous system a steadier signal.

## Can you lower the intensity of hangxiety right now?

Try **extended-exhale breathing** for two minutes. This is not about forcing yourself to feel calm. It is a way to make your exhale a little longer than your inhale, which many people find settling when they feel activated.

1. **Sit somewhere supported.** Put both feet on the floor, or lie on your side if sitting feels unpleasant. Unclench your jaw if you can.

2. **Breathe in gently through your nose for 3 seconds.** Keep it small and easy. A deep breath can feel uncomfortable when you are anxious or nauseated.

3. **Breathe out slowly for 5 seconds.** Let the exhale be soft, as though you are cooling hot tea. Do not push all the air out.

4. **Repeat for 10 rounds.** If counting makes you more tense, drop the numbers and simply let the out-breath be slightly longer than the in-breath.

5. **Name one immediate next step.** For example: “I am going to drink some water,” or “I am going to take a shower.” Keep the task small and concrete.

If you become lightheaded, return to your usual breathing. The goal is comfortable, unforced breathing, not a perfect pattern.

## What is hangxiety?

“Hangxiety” is an informal term for anxiety that shows up during a hangover or the day after drinking. It can include a racing mind, a sense of dread, irritability, poor sleep, physical tension, or replaying conversations from the night before.

It is not a diagnosis. It is a useful shorthand for a real experience that can have several overlapping causes: alcohol's effects wearing off, disrupted sleep, dehydration and stomach discomfort, low energy, worries about what happened while drinking, and your usual anxiety tendencies.

The key point is that the feeling can be both physical and mental. You may be having anxious thoughts, but your body may also be giving your brain plenty of uncomfortable signals to interpret.

## Why does anxiety after drinking happen?

Alcohol initially changes signaling in the brain in ways that can feel sedating or relaxing. In broad terms, it increases the activity of GABA, a calming neurotransmitter system, and reduces activity in glutamate, an excitatory system. As alcohol levels fall, the balance can shift back in the other direction. That rebound can leave some people feeling more alert, restless, or on edge than usual.

This is a simplified explanation, not a complete account of any one person's hangxiety. Alcohol affects many brain and body systems, and effects vary with the amount and timing of drinking, sleep, food, medications, health conditions, and individual sensitivity. Reviews of alcohol's effects on brain systems describe these changing inhibitory and excitatory effects, but they cannot predict exactly how anxious one person will feel after a particular night (Oscar-Berman & Marinković, 2007).

Sleep is another major piece. Alcohol may make it easier to fall asleep at first, but it can disrupt sleep later in the night. Less restorative sleep can make emotions feel sharper and make it harder to put worries in perspective the next day.

Mornings can be especially difficult because the body normally has a rise in cortisol around waking, often called the cortisol awakening response. Cortisol is involved in helping the body mobilize for the day. That normal morning shift is not proof that something is wrong, but if you are already sleep-deprived, dehydrated, and feeling the rebound from alcohol, you may notice your body's activation more intensely. Research supports the existence of a cortisol rise after waking, while the exact role it plays in an individual's hangxiety is less certain (Clow et al., 2010).

Finally, hangovers themselves are unpleasant. Thirst, nausea, headache, a faster heartbeat, and fatigue can resemble sensations people associate with anxiety. Your brain may then start asking, “Why do I feel like this?” and land on alarming explanations.

## Why can hangxiety make thoughts feel so convincing?

When you feel physically unsettled, the mind often tries to explain it. This is especially true if you are already prone to anxiety or social worry.

The morning-after mind may review messages, conversations, or decisions with unusual harshness. Some reflection can be useful later, when you are rested. But in the middle of a hangover, your brain may be working with poor sleep, discomfort, and a heightened threat response. That is not an ideal state for reaching sweeping conclusions about your relationships, character, or future.

Try a temporary rule: **do not make major judgments before you have eaten, hydrated, rested, and given your body time to settle.** You do not have to decide that your worries are false. You can simply postpone the verdict.

A grounding sentence can help: “I am having an anxious morning after drinking. I can review this later, with more information.”

## What should you do after the breathing exercise?

Keep the rest of your approach boring and gentle. Drink water or another nonalcoholic fluid as tolerated, eat something simple if you can, and reduce stimulation. A shower, fresh air, or a brief slow walk may feel better than lying in bed scrolling through old messages.

Be cautious with caffeine. It may help some people feel less tired, but it can also increase jitters, a racing heart, and anxious feelings, especially after poor sleep. If you choose to have it, consider a smaller amount than usual and have food and fluids first. Read more about [caffeine and anxiety](/blog/caffeine-and-anxiety).

It is also reasonable to avoid using more alcohol to quiet the feeling. Drinking again can temporarily change how you feel, but it may prolong the cycle and make it harder to notice whether symptoms are worsening.

If you want a quick, practical plan beyond breathing, see [how to calm down fast](/blog/how-to-calm-down-fast-ranked). Choose one action, then give it a few minutes before adding another.

## How long does hangover anxiety last?

For many people, hangxiety improves as the hangover and sleep disruption improve, often over the rest of the day or within a day or two. There is no universal timeline. It may last longer after heavier drinking, repeated nights of poor sleep, or when alcohol has stirred up an existing anxiety problem.

If anxiety after drinking happens often, feels increasingly intense, leads you to drink to cope, or interferes with work, relationships, or sleep, it is worth discussing with a healthcare professional. Support can be practical and nonjudgmental. Here is more on [when to see a professional](/blog/when-to-see-a-professional).

## When is hangxiety not something to manage alone?

Seek urgent medical care for severe or concerning symptoms, such as chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, a seizure, severe confusion, hallucinations, or repeated vomiting that prevents you from keeping fluids down.

If you drink heavily or very regularly and develop shaking, sweating, agitation, confusion, hallucinations, or seizures after cutting down or stopping, get urgent medical help. Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous, and it is not something to manage with a breathing exercise alone.

## What can you take from this morning?

Hangxiety does not require a moral verdict. It is information about how alcohol, sleep, your body, and your stress system may be interacting right now.

For today, make the goal smaller: lengthen the exhale, take care of basic needs, and delay big conclusions. When you feel more settled, you can decide whether there is anything you want to change about future drinking, social plans, sleep, or support.
